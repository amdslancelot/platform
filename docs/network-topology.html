Probe type, and why it turned out not to matter here. Probe mechanism decides whether a NetworkPolicy could break health checking at all: httpGet is a real TCP connection opened from the node, so in principle it is exposed to ingress default-deny; exec runs inside the container via the runtime and touches no network, so it is immune by construction. gelp and transigen probe httpGet :3000, snoopy probes httpGet :8080 on /health and /ready, lans-h-site declares no probe at all, and postgres uses exec pg_isready. Three of those four probe definitions live in the app repos, not here.

Tested 2026-07-29: on this cluster none of them is affected. A throwaway pod with an httpGet readiness probe, under default-deny ingress with no allowance at all, stayed Ready and kept logging kube-probe/1.36 200s — while the control in the same test, a pod-to-pod connection to a policied app pod, was refused. kube-router enforces ingress between pods and exempts host-originated traffic. So the probe rules are precautionary; they would become load-bearing on a CNI swap, a kube-router upgrade, or probing from a second node.
